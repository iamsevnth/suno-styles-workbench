# Suno Workbench v5.8.0: Tempo-Named Genres, Prompt Length, Research Wording and Rephrase

## Fixed

- **"140" no longer repeats.** Before, prompts could open with "140 BPM 140 UKG" or "140 BPM 140 Bass". Genres whose name carries the tempo now follow one rule:
  - Any two of **140**, **UKG** and **Dubstep** written together form one phrase. It is always written in full as **"140 UKG Dubstep"** and never split. "140 UKG", "UKG Dubstep", "Dubstep UKG", "ukg-dubstep" and "140 dubstep" all become that phrase.
  - The number in the name is the prompt's tempo, so the prompt states no separate BPM.
  - With no tempo in the brief, the first prompt is 140. Sibling prompts vary between 135 and 145 (for example "137 UKG Dubstep").
  - "140 Bass", "Deep 140", "Wubby 140", "140 Grime" and "140 Halftime" follow the same rule.
  - Numbered spellings in a brief keep their genre: "138 Bass", "Deep 138", "140 dubstep" and "Dubstep UKG" are read as 140 Bass, Deep 140 Bass and UKG Dubstep, with the number as the tempo.
  - A tempo the brief names always wins.
    - "138 ukg dubstep" gives 138.
    - Outside 135–145 (for example 150 BPM), the name drops its number ("UKG", "UK bass"), the prompt writes "150 BPM", and a note explains why.
  - Only the first tempo-named genre in a prompt carries the number, so the number never repeats a few words later.
  - Fix mode applies the same rule to a pasted prompt before fitting its length.
  - Only a plan with a tempo-named genre may carry its tempo in a genre name; every other prompt still has to write "NNN BPM".
  - Two numbered tempo-named genres in one prompt count as stating the tempo twice.
  - The Prompt Fidelity Inspector reads a brief's "138 UKG Dubstep" as a 138 BPM target, as generation does.
  - The checks (including those for AI wording) now treat a numbered name beside a separate BPM as a contradiction.
- History entries keep their Instrumental and Length settings across reloads and exports.
- Three phrase-bank entries that said "140" in production wording ("nocturnal 140 club-system production" and two others) no longer carry the number.

## Added

- **Prompt Length** (Create mode):
  - **Detailed** (800–975 characters, the default and the previous behavior).
  - **Tight** (600–850 characters).
  - **Vocal share cap.** The vocal description takes at most **25%** of a Detailed prompt and **15%** of a Tight one. When a long vocal description doesn't fit, planned wording goes first, then the brief's own descriptors. The effects the brief asked for (autotune, slapback delay) stay.
  - The Instrumental toggle works the same at either length.
  - Each result shows a length report, for example "Tight · 742 characters · vocals 11% (cap 15%)".
- **Rephrase · no AI.** Each result has a Rephrase button.
  - It writes new wording for the same musical plan with the Workbench engine. The phrases and musical choices (vocal details, the final-drop change) stay; only sentence frames, connectors and order change.
  - New wording is shown only if it passes the same checks as the original and keeps at least as many of the brief's words.
  - It runs instantly in every browser.
  - It is off for A/B Lab pairs, which differ only in bass articulation.
  - New wording stays inside the chosen length's window.
  - The wording a card shows (rephrased or from your assistant) survives a reload or a page change, like the rest of the results.
  - It is labeled **Rephrased by the Workbench engine · no AI · passed the checks**. **Show Original** brings the first wording back.
- **Reword with your own AI assistant.** This works in every browser, including Brave and Edge.
  - Websites can't call Brave Leo, Microsoft Copilot or other assistants, so this is copy and paste:
    1. **Copy Request** copies the rewording rules and the prompt.
    2. You paste the request into the assistant you choose.
    3. You paste its answer back and click **Check Answer**.
  - The answer is used only if it passes the Workbench checks. It is labeled **AI wording from your assistant**.
  - The request and the check use the wording the card shows (original or rephrased). On-device **Reword with AI** does the same.
  - While a card shows an assistant's wording, the results header says **AI wording on N results**. **Download All Prompts** saves the wording each card shows.
  - AI wording is held to the same vocal share cap, measured the same way for the original and the answer.
  - AI wording (from your assistant or from on-device AI) must keep at least 80% of the result's own musical details, not only the brief's words. It may not add a sound, instrument or effect the original didn't have.
  - The panel says plainly that what you paste into an assistant goes to that company's service. The Workbench sends nothing.

## Changed: wording from the 2026-11-23 research

The research is in `docs/research/advanced-prompt-engineering-research-2026-11-23.md`.

- **Output order.** Prompts follow the research's order:
  1. Identity
  2. Groove
  3. How the hook and bass interact
  4. Texture
  5. Sections
  6. Two or three mix priorities
- **Call and response.** In bass music, the hook and the bass share one sentence ("The hook sits on X, with Y answering in the gaps between kick accents") instead of two unconnected lists. The first bass phrase in native bass prompts answers the hook.
- **What the final drop changes.** A bass-music arc with a drop now says what the final drop changes, tied to the hook and bass ("a final drop that revoices the hook over displaced bass answers").
  - An arc whose later drop already says how it differs ("harder second drop", "switch-up drop with rolling hats") keeps its own wording. A bare "second drop" is replaced by the change.
  - The line goes before an outro, never after it.
  - Song genres, drill and rap get no drop wording.
  - Nothing the brief excludes is written: "no hook" gets no hook wording, and "no hats" gets no hat wording.
- **Mix priorities.** Prompts carry at most two or three mix priorities and no mastering phrases.
- **Trimming order.** When a prompt is over its window, decoration goes first (production labels, extra instruments, sound design, a third mix note). The groove, the hook-and-bass relationship and the drop progression go last. A cut arc keeps its opening and its drop stages.
- **AI wording.**
  - On-device AI wording no longer assumes Chrome: Microsoft Edge's preview builds (Canary and Dev) are mentioned alongside Chrome.
  - AI Assist always points to Rephrase and the own-assistant option.
  - The Privacy Policy covers both.
