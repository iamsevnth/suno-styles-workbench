# Song Editor Techniques

## 14.1 Iterative Section Surgery in the Suno Song Editor
Rarely does a producer need to discard a great render just because one verse stumbled over a
word or the second drop lacked impact. Using the Suno Song Editor (Suno Song Editor
Documentation and Replacing Sections in Song Editor), you can pair Suno Workbench's variant
rotation with surgical timeline operations:

 Song Editor Operation      Precision Workflow with Suno Workbench

 Replace Section            Select a 4-to-16 bar region on the waveform where a lyric mispronunciation
 (Inpainting)               or weak drop occurred. If fixing a vocal line, paste the phonetically respelled
                            line from Lyrics Studio. If upgrading a drop, temporarily swap the Styles field
                            to Variant #3 (Hybrid/Contrast Lead) while keeping the same BPM so the
                            new drop hits harder than the surrounding verses.

 Extend (From               Place the playhead just before an abrupt ending or at the end of Drop 1.
 Timestamp)                 Paste your compiled Workbench prompt into Styles, enter [Breakdown] ->
                            [Build] -> [Final Drop] -> [Outro] in Lyrics, and extend cleanly at the locked
                            integer BPM.

 Crop & Fade Out            Trim dead air or trailing noise after the final reverb tail and apply a smooth
                            2-to-4 bar master fade out so exported WAV files land cleanly on bar
                            boundaries.

 Remaster (v6 Upgrade)      Take an older v4.5 or v5 generation with great songwriting and run
                            Remaster in v6 with your compiled 952-character Workbench prompt in
                            Styles to upgrade sub-bass transient definition and stereo separation.

 Covers & Personas          Save a standout vocal performance as a Persona or Voice, then paste a
                            completely different Suno Workbench genre recipe (e.g., shifting from
                            Emotional R&B to 140 UKG Trap) to build a cohesive multi-track EP with a
                            consistent lead vocalist.






## 14.2 Advanced 12-Track Stem Separation vs. 2-Stem Extraction
As documented in Suno Stem Extraction Guide, Suno offers two tiers of stem extraction from any
generated track:

   2-Stem Extraction (Vocals + Instrumental): Splits the track into Lead/Backing Vocals and Full
    Instrumental. Ideal for quick DJ acapellas, vocal chop resampling, or instrumental sync
    licensing versions.
   Advanced 12-Track Stem Separation: Deconstructs the render into up to 12 discrete WAV
    stems: (1) Lead Vocals, (2) Backing Vocals / Ad-libs, (3) Drums (Full / Kick / Snare), (4)
    Percussion / Hats, (5) Bass / Sub / 808, (6) Electric / Acoustic Guitars, (7) Keys / Piano / Organ,
    (8) Synths / Pads / Leads, (9) Strings / Orchestral, (10) Brass / Winds, (11) Sound FX / Risers,
    and (12) Residual Ambience.
Why do Suno Workbench prompts separate into dramatically cleaner 12-track stems than casual
comma-tag prompts? Because Sentence 3 (Bass), Sentence 4 (Harmony/Palette), Sentence 5
(Vocals), and Sentence 7 (Mix carving) explicitly instruct Suno's synthesis engine to leave low-mid
spectral headroom and mono-anchor the sub below 90 Hz—preventing the spectral overlap that
causes stem bleed during neural source separation.
