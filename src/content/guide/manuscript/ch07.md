# Vocal Budgeting & Voice Lexicon

## 7.1 Why Vocal Token Budgeting Is Critical in Suno v6
One of the most important discoveries encoded into Suno Workbench is that Suno's acoustic
attention layer weights vocal adjectives disproportionately higher than instrumental adjectives. If


a 950-character Styles prompt devotes 350 characters to describing vocal timbre, vibrato,
breathiness, harmonies, ad-libs, and formants, Suno v6 will push the vocal +4 dB above the mix
and compress the bass drop into a background bed—even in genres like Dubstep, Riddim, or
Festival Trap where the drop is supposed to be the centerpiece.

Conversely, in Emotional R&B, Trapsoul, Gospel, or Melodic Rap, under-specifying the vocal
results in flat, robotic phrasing. Suno Workbench solves this by implementing a strict 5-Tier Vocal
Budgeting Engine across plan.ts, lexicon-vocals.ts, and render.ts:

## 7.2 Architecture of the Paired Male/Female Gender-Swap Lexicon
In src/lib/suno/lexicon-vocals.ts, vocal phrases are engineered using a symmetrical dual-gender
architecture (VOCAL_PAIRS: VocalPair[], containing 23 paired rows) alongside gender-exclusive
vocal banks (VOCAL_MALE_ONLY with 10 entries, VOCAL_FEMALE_ONLY with 10 entries), 15
universal vocal modifiers (VOCAL_MODIFIERS), and genre-specific duet/pair mappings
(VOCAL_GENRE_PAIRS).

Why use paired male/female swap rows? When a producer switches the Vocal Override lock from
'Female' to 'Male' (or 'Duet') on the same seed, pickVocalPhrase() selects the exact corresponding
index in VOCAL_PAIRS—preserving the exact emotional intensity, microphone intimacy, and
phrasing style while adapting the vocal register (e.g., swapping 'silky alto' to 'warm baritone' or
'feathered soprano' to 'velvet tenor'). All 23 verbatim VOCAL_PAIRS, 10 Male-Only phrases, 10
Female-Only phrases, and 15 Vocal Modifiers are cataloged in full in Appendix C.
