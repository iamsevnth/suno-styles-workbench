## 13.2 Why Suno v6's 'Variety' Slider MUST Stay at 0 (Off)

      CRITICAL SUNO v6 RULE — KEEP VARIETY AT 0 (OFF) WHEN USING SUNO
      WORKBENCH
      Suno v6 introduced a 'Variety' control in the Create panel with discrete stops at 0 (Off), 25,
      50, 75, and 100. What does Variety actually do under the hood? When Variety is set above 0,
      Suno's server-side preprocessor rewrites and mutates your Styles prompt before feeding it
      to the audio transformer so that the two generated clips sound more different from each
      other.
      Because Suno Workbench already compiles 3 distinct, mathematically verified 925–975
      character prompts with zero negations and exact vocal budgeting, turning Suno's Variety
      slider above 0 corrupts your carefully engineered prose! Always leave Suno's Variety slider
      at 0 (Off) and obtain structural variety by pasting Variant #1, Variant #2, and Variant #3
      across separate runs.




## 13.3 Why 'Personalize (My Taste)' Should Be Turned Off for Hybrid
Prompts
Introduced in Suno v5.5 and carried into v6, the 'Personalize (My Taste)' toggle biases every
generation toward the historical acoustic centroid of tracks you have previously liked or created.
While helpful for casual 3-word prompts, leaving Personalize (My Taste) ON when pasting a Suno
Workbench prompt pulls the model back toward your old habits and resists bold cross-cluster
hybrids (such as Ritual Bass Trap × Deep Dubstep or 140 UKG × Gospel Choir). Turn Personalize
OFF whenever you want faithful execution of a compiled Workbench card.

## 13.5 Suno v6 Max Mode (20 Credits vs. 10 Credits)
In Suno v6, standard generation costs 10 credits per pair (5 credits per clip) and caps initial
generation length at 2 minutes. Enabling Max Mode in Suno v6 costs 20 credits per generation

(10 credits per clip) and unlocks three critical professional capabilities: (1) full-length song
generation beyond 2 minutes (up to 4+ minutes), (2) v6 Covers of existing tracks or uploaded
audio, and (3) v6 Voices / Persona conditioning. Whenever your compiled Suno Workbench
prompt includes a full 6-part arrangement arc (Intro -> Verse -> Build -> Drop 1 -> Bridge -> Final
Drop -> Outro), enable Max Mode so Suno v6 does not rush or truncate the second half of the
arrangement.
